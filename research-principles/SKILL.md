# Apply balanced research principles

Consider the principles and disciplinary lenses broadly, then select and combine
the techniques that best fit the task, including cross-domain perspectives that
may reveal blind spots or enable useful reframing.
Use them to guide the investigation, evaluate evidence and reasoning, choose
appropriate methods, and review the resulting methods, claims, and conclusions
for unsupported assumptions, unacknowledged limitations, and material violations.

Cross-domain research criteria: Research Question, Evidence, Inference, Methodological Fit, Methodological Pluralism, Uncertainty, Transparency, Research Ethics, Reflexivity, Epistemic Humility.
Research integrity: Honesty, Rigour, Reliability, Accountability, Care, Respect, Open Communication.

Domain lenses:
Natural Sciences: Measurement, Experimental Design, Statistical Inference, Replication.
Formal and Computational Sciences: Formalization, Proof, Logic, Computability, Complexity, Model Checking.
Engineering and Technology: Requirements Engineering, Systems Engineering, Modeling and Simulation, Verification and Validation, Safety Engineering, Trade-off Analysis.
Medical and Health Sciences: Clinical Evidence, Risk–Benefit Analysis, Patient Safety, Diagnostic Validity, Evidence-Based Practice.
Agricultural and Veterinary Sciences: Field Trials, Ecological Context, Animal Welfare, Biosecurity, One Health.
Social Sciences: Operationalization, Measurement Validity, Sampling, Causal Inference, Qualitative Inquiry, Mixed Methods, Positionality.
Humanities and the Arts: Hermeneutics, Source Criticism, Contextualization, Close Reading, Conceptual Analysis, Argument Reconstruction, Historiography, Reception History, Practice-Based Research.
Philosophy: Conceptual Analysis, Argument Reconstruction, Validity and Soundness, Principle of Charity, Exegesis, Critique, Historical Contextualism.
History: Historiography, Provenance, Periodization, Triangulation, Reception History.
Literary Studies: Narratology, Formalism, Genre Analysis, Intertextuality, Reception Theory.
Anthropology: Ethnography, Participant Observation, Positionality, Thick Description, Emic and Etic Perspectives.

# Scope

- Research questions, study designs, literature reviews, investigations, and conclusions
- Quantitative, qualitative, historical, interpretive, computational, and mixed-method inquiry
- Claims, arguments, sources, data, models, context, competing explanations, and research limits
